## Overview

### Spotify knows my taste. Maybe a little too well.

It's where most of my music lives. It's great at giving me more of what I already like, and surprisingly bad at helping me find something new.

## Problem

### Spotify keeps playing me songs I already know...

I open Discover Weekly hoping for something new, and a lot of it sounds like music I've already heard. I start a radio station from one song, and a few tracks later I'm back in my own library.

### ...because it's doing its job a little too well.

Spotify wants me to keep listening, and the safest way to do that is to play what I already like. Every replay teaches it I want more of the same, so my listening slowly shrinks into a loop. Radio is built to stay close to where I started. Discovery needs the opposite.

## Explorations

#### Exploration 1

### A dial from Familiar to New

One control on radio that slides from songs I know to songs I've never heard. Turn it all the way to New, and Spotify never plays something I've already played.

It's clean, but it's just a setting. People open Spotify to listen, not to adjust controls. I'd set it once and forget it was there.

#### Exploration 2

### Friends' first listens

A feed of songs my friends heard for the first time this week, so discovery comes from people instead of an algorithm.

I love that it's human, but it depends on friends being active and having taste I trust. For most people, the feed would be empty.

#### What both taught me

### Discovery shouldn't be a setting or a feed. It should be something I do.

The best starting point is a song I already love, because that's where I already am.

## Solution

#### Rabbit hole

### Pick any song. Every next song takes one step further away.

The first few still feel like the song I picked. Ten songs in, I'm somewhere I've never been, and I can see exactly how I got there.

## How it works

#### Entry point

### One tap, right next to shuffle.

Rabbit hole is a way of listening, like shuffle or repeat, so it lives with them in the player. It always starts from whatever is playing right now.

#### The journey

### Each song moves a little further.

A different artist with the same sound, then that sound from another decade, then a new genre that shares something with the last song. It never plays anything I've already heard.

#### What "one step" means

### Every song keeps something from the last one, and changes one thing.

Spotify already understands every song in three ways: how it sounds (tempo, energy, acoustic or electronic), where it comes from (genre, scene, decade), and who listens to it (people who play this also play that). A step changes just one of those and keeps the others.

Starting from a soft bossa nova song, one step might keep the sound and the era but move to a different Brazilian artist. The next keeps that jazz feel but crosses to Ethiopian jazz from the same decade. The next keeps the hypnotic rhythm but lands in desert blues. Each move makes sense on its own, even though the end feels far from the start.

### Three rules keep it from feeling random.

**Each song only has to connect to the one before it.** Not to the song I started with. That's what lets ten small steps add up to somewhere completely new.

**Distance from the start only goes up.** If the next song would pull me back toward where I began, it gets skipped. Otherwise it slowly turns back into radio.

**Nothing I've already heard.** Every song in a rabbit hole is new to me, or the whole point is lost.

### My skips decide how big a step is.

If I skip a song in the first few seconds, that step was too far, so the next one is smaller. If I listen all the way through, the next step can reach a little further. The feature learns my comfort zone while I'm using it, without asking me to set anything.

#### The trail

### Every song I pass through stays on the list, so I can see how far I've gone.

The page shows where I started and where I am now. If I land on something I love, I tap that song and branch off from there.

#### Saving it

### When I'm done, the trail becomes a playlist.

I can come back to it later, or send the whole journey to a friend.

## Reflection

### The obvious fix is usually a setting. The better fix is usually an action.

My first idea was the dial, because it answered the complaint directly. Rabbit hole works better because it starts from something I'm already doing: listening to a song I like.

### The hardest part is the part nobody sees.

How far is one step? Too small and it feels like radio. Too big and it feels random. Letting skips set the step size is my best answer, but getting it right would take real testing with real listeners.
