## What I noticed

I love finding new music. But lately Spotify keeps handing me songs I already know.

I open Discover Weekly hoping for something new, and a lot of it is stuff I've heard before, or stuff that sounds exactly like it. Radio is even worse. I start a station from one song, and a few tracks later I'm back in my own library.

## Why it happens

I don't think the algorithm is broken. I think it's doing its job a little too well.

Spotify wants me to keep listening, and the safest way to do that is to play things I already like. Every time I replay a song, it learns I want more of the same. So my listening slowly shrinks into a loop.

Radio is built to stay close to where I started. That's great when I want more of the same. But when I want to discover something, I need the opposite: something that moves away from where I started.

## The idea

**Rabbit hole.** Pick any song I love and tap "Go down the rabbit hole."

> Every next song takes one step further from where I started.

The first few still feel like the song I picked. Ten songs in, I'm somewhere I've never been. And I can see exactly how I got there.

## How it works

It starts from one tap, on any song, anywhere in the app.

Each track moves a little further away. Maybe a different artist with the same sound, then the same sound from a different decade, then a different genre that shares something with the last song. It never plays a song I've already heard.

As I go, a trail builds at the top of the screen, one dot per song. I can see how far I've traveled. If I hit something I love, I can tap that dot and branch off from there instead.

When I'm done, I can save the whole trail as a playlist. My rabbit hole becomes something I can come back to, or send to a friend.

## What I considered

**A Familiar to New dial.** One control on radio that goes from songs I know to songs I've never heard. It's clean, but it's a setting. It changes how Spotify picks songs without giving me anything to explore.

**Friends' first listens.** A feed of songs my friends heard for the first time this week. I like that it's human, but it only works if my friends are active and have taste I trust.

Rabbit hole won because it turns discovery into something I do, not something I wait for. And the trail makes it feel like a journey instead of a playlist.

## Open questions

How far is one step? Too small and it feels like radio. Too big and it feels random. Getting that distance right is the whole product.

And does Spotify even want this? Unfamiliar songs probably mean more skips in the short term. My bet is it's worth it. People who keep finding music they love have a reason to stay.
